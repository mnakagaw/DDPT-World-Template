## Taiz numeric-field audit and conflict

The yearbook's PDF page 39 prints **2024 population estimates** of 4,231,600 for Taiz, 2,158,100 male and 2,073,500 female, and 604,537 households. PDF page 42, Table 3, prints 23 district rows for 2023 and 2024 with total/male/female columns. Page 43, Table 4, prints 23 district households, rural and urban for 2024. The private `TAIZ_2024_YEARBOOK_DISTRICT_FIELDS.csv` records all 207 field positions across those two tables, including explicit blank cells for dashes; `TAIZ_2024_YEARBOOK_AUDIT.json` records the checks. The table titles, numeric columns, row names and footnote were visually checked on the rendered originals.

- Table 3's 23 printed 2024 district totals sum to **4,231,758**, 158 above its printed 4,231,600 Taiz total. Its 2023 district totals sum to 4,113,515, 115 above the printed 4,113,400. Across the two years, eight district/year rows have male + female unequal to their row total. The summed 2024 male and female district columns exceed their printed Table 3 totals by 61 and 96 respectively.
- Page 39 labels **male 2,158,100; female 2,073,500**. Table 3 on page 42 labels its total row **male 2,073,500; female 2,158,100**. This is a source conflict, not a translation choice to resolve silently. No sex-specific estimate is adopted.
- Table 4's 23 district household totals sum to **604,539**, two above the printed 604,537 total; rural columns sum to 444,022, two above the printed 444,020; urban columns sum to the printed 160,517. Ten rural/urban district cells contain a dash. The table footnote defines a dash as unavailable from the source, so it is **missing, not observed zero**, even where row arithmetic would imply zero.

These discrepancies are small but source-visible. No district value, computed governorate sum, sex split or household rural/urban category is adopted until the source conflict is resolved and the 23 district names are linked to official codes and a versioned boundary. The 2004 census areas, 2017 `geoBoundaries` polygons and 2024 Taiz reporting areas must remain distinct.

## Planning, implementation and budget meaning

The planning-office presentation's PDF page 3 reports 864 plan projects with **estimated** total cost USD 479,629,850. It distinguishes 154 projects labelled implemented in 2024 (estimated cost USD 25,556,782), 78 ongoing (USD 77,448,834), their 232 combined (USD 103,005,616), and 632 remaining (USD 376,624,234). PDF page 4 splits the **combined** 232 by four pillars; 26.9% is 232 / 864 rounded, **not a budget-execution rate or plan-achievement rate**. PDF page 15 identifies 332 **2025 priority** projects with estimated cost USD 207,039,249. Estimated project costs are not actual expenditure, enacted budgets or audit results. The presentation does not by itself verify project-level completion or official evaluation.

The separate 42-page plan identifies its **2024–2026** period and, on PDF page 4 (printed page 3), explicitly says it encompasses **17 named liberated districts**: Al-Mukha, Mawza, Al-Wazi'iyah, Thoubab, Al-Mudhaffar, Al-Qahirah, Salah, Al-Shamaytin, Al-Mawasit, Al-Ma'afir, Jabal Habshi, Al-Misraakh, Mashra'a Wa Hudnan, Sabir Al-Mawadem, Al-Selw, Maqbanah and Sama'e. That is a different coverage from the CSO yearbook's **23** Taiz districts and the 2017 provider governorate polygon. The plan's page 3 methodology mentions preparation for approval as a step; the page 42 appendix images show committee-formation decisions. Neither establishes a final plan approval or applicable current legal status. This document can be listed as an inspected **source**, but cannot be silently attributed to the whole governorate or its 2017 reference polygon. Page 5 lists earlier evidence used for the plan; those named sources were not thereby acquired. Separate official budgets, actual expenditure records and evaluations remain unacquired.
