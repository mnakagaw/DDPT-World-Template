Review date: 2026-09-24. Source PDF, hash and first-seven-table method are in [the printed-page review](burkina-faso-rgph-first-tables-review-2026-09-24.md). Physical PDF pages 99, 122, 216 and 217 were rendered and visually inspected; their printed report pages are 75, 98, 192 and 193. The source is INSD's 2019 RGPH statistical-tables volume. These are **three additional decisions on previously unreviewed tables** and a visual cross-check on one previously selected table, not four new adopted indicators.

| Table, PDF page | Printed column meaning and geography | Decision |

| V.23, 99 | Burkina Faso, RGPH 2019 versus 2006; immigrant and emigrant counts by **different migration concepts**: lifetime, recent, intercensal, and last foreign residence. The recent category shows 45,517 immigrants and 79,191 emigrants for 2019; the printed balance is -33,674. Other concept cells are `NA`/`NC`. | Deferred. The concepts, reference windows and populations differ. No regional values are in this table, and no migrant total or recent-change series is inferred from the mixed rows. Preserve `NA`/`NC` as missing or not comparable, not zero. |
| VI.5, 122 | Burkina Faso, 2019; compulsory-school-age children 6-16, school-attendance rate by urban/rural and male/female/combined. National combined value is 45.9%; urban 67.1%, rural 39.6%. | Cross-check only. The 45.9% national value matches the separately adopted school-status indicator from Table VIII.19. The urban/rural and sex breakdowns are not yet given independent indicator IDs; the similarly worded regional enrolment measures are not automatically treated as identical. |
| IX.22, 216-217 | Burkina Faso, 2019; **combined unemployment** and **ILO/BIT unemployment** rates, each male/female/combined, by 13 historical regions and other characteristics. The national combined unemployment rate is 34.1%, while national ILO/BIT unemployment is 7.1%; Centre ILO/BIT is 9.5%. | Already selected in the earlier 15-table audit. This printed-page check confirms the ILO/BIT column used in the adopted indicator and the need to keep it distinct from combined unemployment. It does not add another indicator. |

A new count indicator would need its own label/denominator and full row check; IX.22 rates must not be overwritten by IX.23 shares. |
